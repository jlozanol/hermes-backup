# YouTube Script Writer

Write video scripts designed for retention: a researched hook, tight pacing, and a clear payoff.

## When to Use

- The user asks to write, outline, or improve a YouTube video script
- The user shares a video topic or title and wants it turned into a script
- Do NOT use for short form video such as TikTok, Reels, or Shorts. The pacing rules are different.

## Quick Reference

- Hook length: 30 seconds max
- Script length: about 150 spoken words per minute of runtime
- Body sections: 3 to 5
- Calls to action: exactly 1
- Markers: [B ROLL] for visuals, [CUT] for scene changes

## Procedure

1. Research before writing. Search YouTube for the video topic and find 3 to 5 recent high-performing videos on it. Note their titles, how they hook viewers, and how they structure the content. Use this context to inform the script.

2. Confirm the basics with the user: target video length, audience, and channel tone. If they have already told you, do not ask again.

3. Write the hook first. The opening 30 seconds must state the payoff of the video and tease the ending. Never open with a greeting or channel intro.

4. Outline the body in 3 to 5 sections. Each section should end with an open loop: a reason to keep watching into the next section.

5. Write the full script in spoken language: short sentences, contractions, and no essay paragraphs. Add [B ROLL] and [CUT] markers where visuals should change.

6. End with exactly one call to action.

## Pitfalls

- Never open with "Hey guys, welcome back" or any channel intro. Retention data shows viewers leave during greetings.

- Do not write for reading; write for speaking. If a sentence sounds stiff when read aloud, rewrite it.

- Do not stack multiple calls to action. Use one, at the end.

- Do not pad the runtime. If the content supports 6 minutes, do not stretch it to 10.

## Verification

- Read the first 30 seconds: does it clearly state what the viewer gets?
- Check the length: roughly 150 spoken words per minute of target runtime
- Confirm every section ends with a reason to keep watching
- Confirm there is exactly one call to action
